## Executive Summary

**Purpose.** This document accompanies the edited draft of the District's Standard Operating Guidelines (`sog-1st-pass.md`). It exists so the Chief can efficiently approve the substantive changes already made, decide a short list of remaining policy questions, and see the open items that need information from the department before the SOG can be finalized.

**What was changed in the draft.** Two substantive edit passes were made against the baseline (`sog-1st-pass.md`):

1. **Jurisdictional corrections.** The baseline was adapted from a Wisconsin template and still cited Wisconsin agencies, statutes, and offices in six places. All six were replaced with the correct Washington authorities (WAC 296-305, RCW 49.17, RCW 49.60, the Washington State Human Rights Commission, and the EEOC Seattle Field Office). Each replacement is source-verified and documented in the Review Notes section of the draft.
2. **Mandatory-language standardization (Stage A).** The draft was aligned to a single mandatory modal — **shall** — for all binding requirements, matching the style guide and long-standing fire-service convention. Roughly forty `must` statements and seven directive `will` statements were converted; ten ALL-CAPS emphasis blocks were rewritten in normal case with bold retained. Verbatim OSHA regulatory text was left untouched.

Formatting cleanup (broken numbered lists, ALL-CAPS headings, stray HTML, hidden characters) was also completed. That work is mechanical and does not require the Chief's sign-off; the details are in the Review Notes section of the draft for the tech writer's reference.

**What the Chief needs to decide.** Three short lists:

- **Punch List A — Sign-off on Wisconsin → Washington replacements** (Section 1 below). One-line confirm-or-correct for each of the six items.
- **Punch List B — Stage B modal classification** (Section 2 below). Five safety-critical `should` statements where the recommendation is to promote to **shall** based on OSHA, WAC, or NFPA authority. Everything else in the "should" category can remain as-is unless the Chief flags something specific.
- **Punch List C — Permissive-as-mandatory rewrites** (Section 3 below). Two short sentences currently phrased as "may only / no ... may" that should be rewritten as "shall / shall not." Editorial mechanics only, but a policy read.

**Open items.** Independent of the decisions above, four pieces of information are needed from the department before adoption: two PLHCP identifications, one truncated sentence, and two contact-verification checks. Section 4 lists them.

### 1.2 Mandatory-language standardization (Stage A)

The style guide adopts **shall** as the District's single mandatory-requirement modal, consistent with NFPA-family drafting conventions used across the fire service (NFPA *Manual of Style for Technical Committee Documents*). The baseline mixed **must**, **will**, and ALL-CAPS emphasis for the same purpose. Stage A converted the mechanically clear cases:

- ~40 body-prose `must` → `shall` (excluding OSHA-quoted text).
- ~7 clearly directive `will` → `shall`, only inside numbered arrival/incident-action procedures and a few explicit role-directives.
- 10 ALL-CAPS emphasis blocks → normal-case bold. **No new boxed Warning/Caution blocks were introduced;** if the Chief wants any of the former ALL-CAPS items formalized as warnings (with **must**), that is a design decision for a follow-up pass.

**Not changed:** four verbatim OSHA quotes from 29 CFR 1910.134 Appendix C (the medical evaluation questionnaire), which must be reproduced as written; and two grammatical-trigger uses of `must` ("when the apparatus must be moved…") where the mandatory clause is elsewhere in the sentence.

**Chief's action.** No decision required for Stage A — reported here for transparency. Stage B (the `should` classification) is the decision request; see Section 2.

## Section 2 — Punch List: Stage B Modal Classification

**What this list is.** The current draft contains ~80 `should` statements. Most are genuinely recommended practice and can stay as **should**. Five, however, describe **life-safety practices that OSHA, WAC, or the applicable NFPA standard treats as mandatory**. Leaving them as *should* creates a mismatch between the SOG and the governing authority — a foreseeable liability exposure. The recommendation for each is to promote to **shall** (or **shall not**).

**How to read the list.** For each item: the current sentence, where it lives, the recommended change, and the external authority that supports the change. Every recommendation is grounded in an OSHA regulation, a Washington regulation, or a nationally recognized consensus standard.

### B-1. Prohibit removing SCBA before atmosphere is verified clear

- **Currently reads (Overhaul, §Structure Fires):** *"Personnel **should not** remove their breathing apparatus until the area is completely cleared of toxic gases."*
- **Recommended:** *"Personnel **shall not** remove their breathing apparatus until the area is verified clear of carbon monoxide and other products of combustion (measured by a calibrated CO/multi-gas meter)."*
- **Authority.** 29 CFR 1910.134(d) (respiratory protection required whenever employees are or may be exposed to atmospheric contaminants above permissible limits). NFPA 1500, Chapter 8 (respiratory protection during overhaul is a mandatory-practice element of the fire-department safety program). *Post-fire overhaul carbon-monoxide exposure* is the specific fire-service application; both federal OSHA and every NFPA edition since the early 2000s treat SCBA-until-verified-safe as a required practice, not a recommended one.

### B-2. Require a Rapid Intervention Team for interior IDLH operations

- **Currently reads (Structure Fires — Scene Safety):** *"There **should** be a 'R.I.T.' in place on any interior operation or any situation where personnel are exposed or could be exposed to any IDLH situation."*
- **Recommended:** *"A Rapid Intervention Team **shall** be in place on any interior structural operation and on any operation where personnel are exposed to an IDLH atmosphere."*
- **Authority.** 29 CFR 1910.134(g)(4) — the federal "two-in / two-out" rule — requires that at least two firefighters stand by outside an IDLH atmosphere, in communication with the interior crew, ready to perform rescue. NFPA 1500 (Chapter 8) and NFPA 1407 (*Standard for Training Fire Service Rapid Intervention Crews*) further codify the RIT requirement for interior structural firefighting. **This is federal law, not a recommendation.**

### B-3. Require immediate MAYDAY notification

- **Currently reads (MAYDAY, §Establishing RIT):** *"Firefighters **should** not delay notification of distress. The Mayday announcement **should** occur as soon as the firefighter thinks that he/she may be in trouble."*
- **Recommended:** *"Firefighters **shall** not delay notification of distress. A Mayday **shall** be announced as soon as the firefighter recognizes he or she may be in trouble."*
- **Authority.** NFPA 1500 (personnel safety and Mayday response) and NFPA 1407 (RIC training) treat the immediate-Mayday standard as a mandatory element of a fire-department safety program. Delayed Mayday reporting is one of the most consistently cited contributing factors in NIOSH Firefighter Fatality Investigation reports; the corrective recommendation in NIOSH reports is uniformly framed as a *requirement*, not a suggestion.

### B-4. Prohibit simultaneous offensive/defensive operations

- **Currently reads (Structure Fires — Confinement/Extinguishment):** *"…a defensive attack **should not** be used when crews are operating on the interior."*
- **Recommended:** *"A defensive attack **shall not** be conducted while crews are operating on the interior. If tactics change from offensive to defensive, all interior crews **shall** be withdrawn and personnel accountability **shall** be confirmed before exterior streams are used."*
- **Authority.** NFPA 1500 (offensive-to-defensive strategy transitions must be controlled by the IC with accountability) and NFPA 1561 (*Standard on Emergency Services Incident Management System and Command Safety*, mandatory IC control of strategy changes). This is one of the highest-consequence fireground rules; "flowing water into a structure with crews inside" appears repeatedly in NIOSH LODD investigations.

### B-5. Require search-room marking during primary/secondary search

- **Currently reads (Structure Fires — Rescue):** *"A primary and secondary search shall be conducted at all structure fires. During search all rooms **should** be marked by some means to indicate that the particular room has been searched."*
- **Recommended:** *"…During search, each room **shall** be marked by an agreed means to indicate that the room has been searched."*
- **Authority.** NFPA 1500 (personnel accountability during interior operations) and the standard fire-service search doctrine reflected in IFSTA's *Essentials of Fire Fighting* and in every state fire-training curriculum. Room marking is the accountability mechanism that makes primary/secondary search auditable; leaving it as *should* undermines the *shall* in the preceding sentence.

### Everything else in the "should" bucket

The remaining ~75 `should` statements read as genuine recommendations — tactical judgment calls, hedged practices ("if available"), or descriptive guidance. Unless the Chief flags a specific one, the recommendation is to **leave them as `should`**. Representative examples that are correctly classified as **should**:

- "A Spotter should always be used when backing, **if available**." (The hedge is legitimate; a spotter is not always physically possible.)
- Rehab hydration volumes and rest intervals (NFPA 1584 recommends but does not mandate specific volumes; departmental discretion is appropriate).
- Wildland tactical guidance on flanking and head-attack sequencing (tactical judgment).
- Overhaul procedural guidance ("a fresh crew should perform overhaul," "particular attention should be given to hidden areas") — best practice, not required practice.

**Chief's action.** For each of B-1 through B-5: approve as recommended, modify the wording, or reject. If you want a full line-by-line audit of the other 75 items, the tech writer can produce one in a follow-up pass.

## Section 3 — Punch List: Permissive-as-Mandatory Rewrites

Two sentences in the baseline are grammatically phrased as *permissions* but operationally are *mandatory restrictions*. Both were left unchanged in the edit pass because rewording them is a policy call, not a formatting fix. Recommended rewrites are minimal.

### C-1. Firefighter training prerequisite

- **Currently reads (Training):** *"No new fire fighter **may** be permitted to participate in structural fire fighting activities or trainings which require the individual to enter or be in close proximity to the building, enclosed structure, vehicle or vessel until that individual has completed required training."*
- **Recommended:** *"A new firefighter **shall not** participate in structural firefighting activities or trainings that require entering or being in close proximity to a building, enclosed structure, vehicle, or vessel until the individual has completed required training."*
- **Authority.** WAC 296-305-05001 (firefighter training and qualification prerequisites) and 29 CFR 1910.156(c) (fire brigade training required before interior structural firefighting). Both are cast in the language of *shall / shall not*; the SOG should match.

### C-2. Apparatus operator qualification

- **Currently reads (Driver's Manual):** *"**Fire apparatus may only be operated** by individuals meeting all of the following requirements:"*
- **Recommended:** *"Fire apparatus **shall** be operated only by individuals who meet all of the following requirements:"*
- **Authority.** WAC 296-305-04501 (driver/operator qualifications). Same rationale as C-1 — match the SOG's mandatory language to the WAC's.

**Chief's action.** Approve, modify, or reject each rewrite. Both are editorial-mechanical changes; no operational content moves.
